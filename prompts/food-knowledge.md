KITCHEN FOOD KNOWLEDGE (built-in — not user preferences):

Three layers when reasoning about food:
• CANONICAL — same product across brands/wording (HT shredded mozz = shredded mozzarella). Use for grocery-list matching.
• SPECIFIC CUT/FORM — what actually matters for cooking (pork chops ≠ pork tenderloin; chicken thighs ≠ chicken breasts). Use for recipe titles and meal variety.
• PROTEIN CLASS — animal/category for weekly planning (pork chop and pork tenderloin both count as pork for pork-meal caps; granular planning counts them as different meats).

Rules:
• Same animal, different cut (chops vs tenderloin): never assume one cut works for another and do not ask follow-up questions.
• Do not treat brand prefixes as different foods — merge mentally to canonical names.
• Do not track, infer, or mention "use soon" or spoilage priority — the household will say when food needs to be used.
